Some key elements are still missing from the mod:

* Earlier playable bookmarks (1st & 2nd Northern wars, Falka's rebellion, The Usurper, Aelirenn’s Rebellion, 6 years war, ...), with an earliest limit with the first settlments following the landing of the Exiles
* A colonization mechanic to reclaim land from the wilderness
* More depth on the humans-vs-elves conflict
* Training of new Witchers
* A darker tone, such as the ravages of war (disease, monsters), curses (transformative, fertility, bloodline), and more moral conflicts with no clear good outcome, etc.
* ...

Should we implement these elements in CK2 or should we consider first porting the mod to CK3 ?

Here are some arguments for both options:

## Continue on CK2

* The CK2 base game is free, making the mod free-to-play for any Witcher fan, even though the CK2 UI from 2012 may now feel a bit old school for new gamers.
* Leverage the time already invested in the current mod. Total conversion mods are very time-consuming, especially map modding.
* Expanding the history with earlier bookmarks is a good investment and would be reusable in any future mods.
* The overall CK3 experience is a bit of a mess: it looks nicer, but the usability and game flow are not as good (notifications, event pacing, AI war behavior, ...). We'd probably need to integrate or be compatible with some re-balancing mods, as the vanilla experience has its pain points.

## Switch effort to CK3

* CK3 has a bigger active player base, which also means more potential contributors.
  * CK3: 26,272 daily peak as of October 2026 - https://steamdb.info/app/203770/charts/#6y
  * CK2: 1,416 daily peak as of October 2026 - https://steamdb.info/app/1158310/charts/#max
* CK3 has many concepts similar to CK2, so the gap shouldn't be too big.
* There is more tooling for AI-assisted development and ad-hoc tools developed thanks to AI, which could make modding more efficient.
* CK3 has some new mechanics that would benefit the mod:
  * Playable witchers would be possible, and landless adventurers should no longer be DLC-locked
  * The stress mechanics would be good for spellcasters.
  * Battle mages could be properly represented via men-at-arms and by countering other battle mages
  * With baronies visible on the map, mage towers would no longer have to be counties and would no longer mess up the de jure drift when occupied by wilderness
  * Situations and struggles could be used to represent the humans-vs-elves conflicts, the white frost & the elder blood, etc.
  * The population mechanic announced with Silk & Silver could help with a colonization mechanic
* Major updates may potentially stop at some point in the next 2 years?, reducing the effort spent on compatibility patching.
  * CK2: 2012-02 (release) -> 2019-10 (last upgrade) = 7.5 years, 11 core/major expansion DLCs
  * CK3: 2020-09 (release) -> 2026-12 (now) = 6 years, 8 core/major expansion DLCs announced so far
* We could ask permission to reuse some work from The Witcher Realms mod
